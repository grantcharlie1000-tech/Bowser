Install in three steps

  1. Open Bowser.exe

    Open it from your Downloads folder. Windows may show a blue SmartScreen box, because Bowser isn't signed yet: choose More info, then Run anyway.

  2. Bowser installs itself

    It moves into %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Bowser, adds Bowser to your desktop and Start menu, and opens. No administrator rights needed. You can delete the file in Downloads.

  3. Bring your things

    Import bookmarks from Chrome, Edge, Brave or Firefox in Settings, and passwords from a CSV your old browser exports.

What you need

  • 64-bit Windows 10 or 11.
  • The WebView2 Runtime. Windows 11 and current Microsoft Edge already include it. If Bowser says it's missing, get the Evergreen runtime from Microsoft.
  • About 10 MB for Bowser itself. Your profile grows with your history and cache.

Staying up to date

  • Automatic. About a minute after Bowser starts, and then at most once an hour, it checks the stable version pinned in the repository.
  • Checked. A new build is downloaded only when its SHA-256 matches the pin, and installs the next time you start Bowser. The main menu offers Restart to update, and your tabs come back.
  • Safe. A build that fails to stay open is rolled back. Your browser data is never touched.
  • Your call. Turn automatic updates off, or check now, in Settings, Updates.

Other ways to get Bowser

  • Every release is on GitHub, with its build details.
  • From a clone of the repository, Open-Bowser.cmd installs the pinned stable build and adds the shortcuts.
  • Build it yourself with Rust and the Visual Studio C++ Build Tools: cargo build --release --locked. See Build and run.
